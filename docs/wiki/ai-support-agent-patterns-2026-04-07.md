# AI Support-Agent Patterns

Date: 2026-04-07
Type: Dated research report

## Why this topic matters

Support is one of the most durable and valuable agent use cases because it sits at the intersection of:

- knowledge retrieval
- workflow routing
- customer communication
- human escalation
- action-taking systems

Many teams first discover the limits of AI products in support, because support forces the system to be:

- grounded
- traceable
- channel-aware
- safe
- measurable

## What support agents are actually for

The best support agents are not just FAQ bots.

They are used for:

- answering common questions
- summarizing cases
- routing issues to the right team
- collecting missing information
- handling account and billing guidance
- creating follow-up tasks or escalations
- preserving continuity across channels

## The strongest design pattern

### Pattern 1. Knowledge + workflow

Support quality comes from two separate but related systems:

- knowledge grounding
- workflow execution

Knowledge answers questions.
Workflow resolves situations.

A support agent that only knows things but cannot route, escalate, or open a follow-up workflow is weaker than it appears.

### Pattern 2. Escalation is a first-class feature

Support systems work better when they can cleanly decide:

- answer now
- ask a follow-up
- route to a human
- create a case/task
- summarize for the next responder

### Pattern 3. Channel continuity matters

Real support does not live in one window. It moves across:

- web chat
- email
- internal task systems
- support consoles
- team chat

The agent should preserve continuity across those surfaces.

## What a strong support agent needs

- grounded knowledge
- clear scope
- escalation rules
- case/task creation
- summaries and handoffs
- auditability
- satisfaction and resolution tracking

## Underground Circle relevance

This category matters because the app is already community-oriented.

The missing product opportunity is a member-facing support/help layer that can:

- answer how-to questions
- route requests
- turn unresolved requests into Feed tasks
- escalate into Rooms or Office flows
- keep history and summaries visible
