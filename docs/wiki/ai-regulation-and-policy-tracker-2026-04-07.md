# AI Regulation And Policy Tracker

Date: 2026-04-07
Type: Dated research report

## Why this matters

AI policy is now a real product constraint, not a side topic.

For builders, regulation and policy affect:

- deployment choices
- documentation needs
- risk classification
- transparency features
- governance requirements
- procurement and enterprise adoption

## The EU AI Act

The EU AI Act is still the most important policy reference point because it is the first major comprehensive legal framework for AI.

Official sources:

- https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
- https://commission.europa.eu/news/ai-act-enters-force-2024-08-01_en
- https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/artificial-intelligence/

What the official materials emphasize:

- a risk-based framework
- different obligations by risk level
- transparency obligations for some systems
- stricter obligations for high-risk systems
- special treatment for general-purpose AI and systemic risk

This matters because it gives product teams a concrete vocabulary for risk and compliance.

## NIST AI RMF

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework remains one of the most useful practical governance references for organizations, especially in the United States.

Official sources:

- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10

What the official materials emphasize:

- managing AI risk across the system lifecycle
- voluntary, practical, organization-level use
- trustworthy and responsible AI

This matters because it is closer to an operational playbook than many headline policy debates.

## OECD AI Principles

The OECD principles remain important because they provide one of the most widely recognized intergovernmental AI governance references.

Official source:

- https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/ai-principles.html

What they emphasize:

- human-centered and trustworthy AI
- rights and democratic values
- practical guidance for policymakers and AI actors

## Why builders should care even if they are not heavily regulated yet

Policy ideas often become product requirements before they become hard law.

Examples:

- transparency
- logging
- human oversight
- risk classification
- incident handling
- documentation

## The practical product lesson

The strongest move is not to wait for a legal team to translate policy into product changes later.

It is to build products with:

- traceability
- approvals
- role boundaries
- artifact logging
- risk-aware capability bundles

These help regardless of the exact future regulatory path.
