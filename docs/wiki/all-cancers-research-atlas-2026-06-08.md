# All Cancers Research Atlas

Date: 2026-06-08

Status: Dated research report. Use this as an educational map, not as medical advice, diagnosis, or a treatment plan. Cancer care depends on the exact diagnosis, stage, pathology, biomarkers, health history, and clinician review.

## Purpose

Cancer is not one disease. It is a large family of diseases in which abnormal cells grow, invade, or spread in ways that can damage organs and body systems. A useful wiki entry needs to help users ask the right questions before they read a paper, compare a treatment, or talk with a clinician.

The safest research shape is:

1. Name the cancer type and body site.
2. Confirm the cell type or histology.
3. Capture stage, grade, and whether the cancer has spread.
4. Capture biomarkers, inherited risk, and molecular test results when relevant.
5. Separate screening, diagnosis, treatment, survivorship, and palliative care.
6. Track source date, source quality, and uncertainty.

## Fast taxonomy

| Cancer family | What it means | Common examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carcinomas | Cancers that start in epithelial tissues lining organs, glands, skin, ducts, and mucosal surfaces. | Breast, lung, colorectal, prostate, pancreatic, liver, stomach, bladder, kidney, thyroid, head and neck, cervical, uterine, ovarian, testicular. |
| Sarcomas | Cancers that start in bone, muscle, fat, blood vessels, connective tissue, or gastrointestinal stromal cells. | Osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, liposarcoma, angiosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, gastrointestinal stromal tumor. |
| Blood and immune cancers | Cancers of blood-forming tissue, lymph nodes, plasma cells, or marrow. | Leukemia, lymphoma, multiple myeloma, myelodysplastic syndromes, myeloproliferative neoplasms. |
| Central nervous system cancers | Tumors that arise in the brain, spinal cord, meninges, or supporting cells. | Glioblastoma, astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma, ependymoma, medulloblastoma, meningioma, primary CNS lymphoma. |
| Skin cancers | Cancers that arise in skin cells, melanocytes, or skin neuroendocrine cells. | Melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, Merkel cell carcinoma, cutaneous lymphoma. |
| Germ cell and embryonal cancers | Cancers arising from reproductive germ cells or early developmental tissues, often in children or young adults. | Testicular germ cell tumor, ovarian germ cell tumor, neuroblastoma, Wilms tumor, retinoblastoma, hepatoblastoma. |
| Endocrine and neuroendocrine cancers | Cancers from hormone-producing organs or neuroendocrine cells. | Thyroid cancer, adrenal cortical carcinoma, pituitary tumors, parathyroid cancer, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor, small bowel neuroendocrine tumor. |
| Rare and site-specific tumors | Less common malignancies that require specialty review. | Mesothelioma, thymoma and thymic carcinoma, penile cancer, vaginal cancer, vulvar cancer, appendix cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, ocular melanoma. |

## How to research any specific cancer safely

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What is the exact diagnosis? | Body site alone is not enough. Histology and subtype can change the whole plan. |
| Has pathology been reviewed? | Second pathology review can matter for rare cancers, sarcomas, lymphomas, and unusual tumors. |
| What is the stage? | Localized, regional, and metastatic disease often have different goals and treatment sequences. |
| What is the grade or risk group? | Some cancers grow slowly and can be monitored; others need urgent treatment. |
| What biomarkers were tested? | Biomarkers can guide targeted therapy, immunotherapy, inherited-risk counseling, and trial matching. |
| Is the goal cure, control, symptom relief, or prevention of recurrence? | The same drug or procedure can mean different things in different settings. |
| What are the patient priorities? | Age, fertility, function, work, caregiving, travel, values, side effects, cost, and access all matter. |
| Is a clinical trial relevant? | Trials can be standard-plus-research options, especially for rare, advanced, or biomarker-defined cancers. |

## Red-flag research rule

For any cancer-related note, flag the content for clinician review if it contains:

- a diagnosis claim from symptoms alone
- a treatment recommendation
- a medication, supplement, or dose instruction
- advice to delay biopsy, imaging, surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or prescribed therapy
- claims that one cure works for all cancers
- claims based only on anecdotes, testimonials, or seller-controlled evidence
